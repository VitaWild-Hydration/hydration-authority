If you are trying to conceive, somebody has already handed you a list. A book, a forum thread, a practitioner, your sister. And the list is always pills. A prenatal, then CoQ10, then inositol, then vitamin D, then an omega, and by the time you are done you have a shelf of bottles and a weekly organizer and no one to tell you what the whole thing adds up to.

Enter: the fertility drink mix.

The right one can cover what that shelf was trying to cover, in a glass of water, in a form you will actually keep taking for the three months a body needs. The wrong one is a sugary packet with one ingredient in it and a fertility word on the label.

This article started with your emails. After our GLP-1 hydration guide, the question I got most often was not about electrolytes. It was from women coming off the medication to try for a baby, and it was some version of: *I cannot face another bottle of pills. What can I drink instead?* Fair question. The prescribing information for the most common GLP-1 says to stop at least two months before a planned pregnancy, which means a lot of you are standing in the supplement aisle right now, a little nauseated by the memory of the last routine, wondering what to buy.

So I did what we do here. I bought the fertility supplements that come as powders and drink mixes, plus the pill routines they are trying to replace, and I spent six weeks stirring, tasting and reading labels. Not to tell you what will get you pregnant. Nobody honest can tell you that. To tell you which routine is actually complete, actually drinkable, and actually priced like something you could keep up for ninety days.

A few things surprised me. Most of the fertility category still sells one ingredient at a time. Half the labels hide their doses inside a proprietary blend. And very few products are built for both jobs a woman trying to conceive is actually doing: supporting the cycle she is in right now, and building the foundation a pregnancy will draw on. Most cover one and quietly leave you to buy the other.

I rated each based on:

- **Taste & Mixability** (can you drink it every day for three months, including the mornings when nothing sounds good)

- **Formula** (does it cover both jobs, the cycle and the foundation, with the forms the research actually used)

- **Label Transparency** (every ingredient and every amount printed, branded forms where it matters, no proprietary blends)

- **Value** (what a complete routine costs per month, not what one bottle costs)

Some were hits, some were half a routine at a full-routine price. Below, my honest rankings, so you can pick one thing, take it every day, and get back to your life.
